bio

One founder. One product. Receipts, not slides.

Julian Torres is the founder of Tribunus and serves as its sole maintainer. He started the company in October 2024 in San Francisco with one conviction: that the next generation of AI infrastructure should be compiled, not improvised — and that the people who run models on their own machines deserve plans, not logs.

Before Tribunus, Julian worked as a software engineer in the San Francisco Bay Area, and as a freelance photographer and designer. That creative background is visible in the company's design language — minimal, monochrome, terminal-honest — and in the care taken with documentation, schema versioning, and the look of every receipt the system produces.

He is currently shipping one product — Tessera Studio, a desktop app for running large language models locally with calibrated per-tensor quantization, native spec-decoding (DFlash / DSpark), and ANE prefill on M-series Macs. It is the only product Tribunus is shipping in 2026. The company does not raise on hype and does not ship on hype; the receipts are the pitch.

"I am one founder, doing one product, and I am not going to ship it badly. If you are a Mac or Linux user, a Swift or C++ engineer, a quantization researcher, an advisor who has shipped before, or an investor who backs technical founders with shipped artifacts — the page that names the ask is on the site." — Julian Torres, founder of Tribunus

Tribunus is the operational expression of that view. Julian is the project's final decision authority on architecture, roadmap, contribution acceptance, and licensing — a deliberate, founder-led model that will be revisited as the project matures. timeline

How Tribunus got here.

2024 — October

Tribunus is founded

Julian Torres founds Tribunus in San Francisco as an independent, founder-led software company. The thesis: the inference + agent stack must be compiled together, not assembled at runtime.

2024 — Q4

First cut: a desktop agent with local inference

Tribunus begins as a derivative of opencode (SST Inc., MIT) and quickly diverges. The desktop shell, plugin system, and capability-scoped tool runtime take shape against the first compute backend.

2025

The compute engine takes over

The inference engine is re-architected around a compile-time planner. Backend admission becomes a 6-check pipeline; the numerical oracle (Apple Silicon FP32) becomes the golden reference.

2026 — H1

The Tessera pivot

The Tribunus fork of llama.cpp is named Tessera and the desktop product is renamed Tessera Studio. The earlier "Tribunus Desktop / Compute / Cell / Federation" stack is consolidated into one product. The Swift app (TesseraStudio) and the C++ fork (tessera) ship from a single repo.

2026 — August

Founders, Inc. application

Julian applies to Founders, Inc. 2026 under the Tribunus identity, with the Tessera Studio live macOS developer preview and the C++ fork of llama.cpp as the shipped artifacts. The company is pre-seed and self-funded.

intellectual lineage

The references that shape Tessera.

Tessera's architecture is not invented from scratch. The team has published a long-form essay — The Tribunus Thesis — that traces the intellectual lineage paper by paper. A few anchors:

Kwon et al., PagedAttention (SOSP 2023). The reframing of inference as memory orchestration. Tessera's KV-cache handling and per-token plan live here.

Mark Miller, Robust Composition. Capability-based security. The plugin runtime, the surface separation between desktop / web / mobile, and the "no ambient authority" rule all derive from this lineage.

Kleppmann et al., Local-First Software. The client is the authority for its own data; sync is eventual and conflict-free; offline is not an afterthought. Tessera Studio runs locally first.

Lattner et al., MLIR (CGO 2021). The progressive-lowering, dialect-composable compiler architecture. Tessera's calibration pipeline is built on a similar progressive-validate pattern.
