bio

Building infrastructure that earns the right to be trusted.

Julian Torres is the founder of Tribunus and serves as its sole maintainer. He started the company in October 2024 in San Francisco with one conviction: that the next generation of AI infrastructure should be compiled, not improvised — and that engineers running agents on their own machines deserve plans, not logs.

Before Tribunus, Julian worked as a software engineer in the San Francisco Bay Area, and as a freelance photographer and designer. That creative background is visible in the company's design language — minimal, monochrome, terminal-honest — and in the care taken with documentation, schema versioning, and the look of every receipt the system produces.

"Agents shouldn't be chat wrappers. They should be compiled systems that produce receipts for what they did, and you should be able to verify every receipt against the source. That's the only way to make them trustworthy enough to give a real codebase to." — Julian Torres, founder of Tribunus

Tribunus is the operational expression of that view. Julian is the project's final decision authority on architecture, roadmap, contribution acceptance, and licensing — a deliberate, founder-led model that will be revisited as the project matures. All Tribunus-specific work is Copyright © 2024–2026 Julian Torres; the project's NOTICE.md preserves the original MIT attribution to SST Inc. for code derived from opencode.

He works with a small, explicit network of contributors and is currently the only full-time person on the project. The company is self-funded, independent, and has not raised outside capital.

timeline

How Tribunus came to be.

2024 — October

Tribunus is founded

Julian Torres founds Tribunus in San Francisco as an independent, founder-led software company. The thesis: the inference + agent stack must be compiled together, not assembled at runtime.

2024 — Q4

First cut: a desktop agent with local inference

Tribunus begins as a derivative of opencode (SST Inc., MIT) and quickly diverges. The desktop shell, plugin system, and capability-scoped tool runtime take shape against the first compute backend.

2025

The compute engine takes over

The inference engine is re-architected around a compile-time planner. Backend admission becomes a 6-check pipeline; the numerical oracle (Apple Silicon FP32) becomes the golden reference. Multi-backend support: CUDA, Metal, Vulkan, ROCm, oneDNN, Level Zero, TT-NN.

2026 — H1

The cell + the federation

The local-first Cell (PGlite + Valkey) lands as the durable authority. The federated control plane enters design. Tribunus opens a company GitHub org at github.com/Tribunus-dev.

2026 — Now

Five execution-platform systems in flight

Plugin manifests & capability declarations, supervised agent execution, local inference providers, collaboration authority, and distributed compute federation — all in active development. AGPLv3 with commercial dual-licensing.

intellectual lineage

The references that shape Tribunus.

Tribunus's architecture is not invented from scratch. The team has published a long-form essay — The Tribunus Thesis — that traces the intellectual lineage paper by paper. A few anchors:

Yann LeCun, A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence (2022). The hierarchical planner / world-model / actor split is the structural template for the Tribunus agent runtime.

Kwon et al., PagedAttention (SOSP 2023). The reframing of inference as memory orchestration. Tribunus's KV-cache compression and per-token plan live here.

Lattner et al., MLIR (CGO 2021). The progressive-lowering, dialect-composable compiler architecture. Tribunus's PhaseIR is not MLIR, but it is MLIR's architecture applied to the inference problem.

Mark Miller, Robust Composition. Capability-based security. The plugin runtime, the surface separation between desktop / web / mobile, and the "no ambient authority" rule all derive from this lineage.

Kleppmann et al., Local-First Software. The client is the authority for its own data; sync is eventual and conflict-free; offline is not an afterthought. The Tribunus Cell model is the local-first applied to AI agents.
